// 03. chemistry

Where the science
meets the code.

An MS in Chemistry is the foundation of how I think as an engineer. I turn R&D data from battery, hydrogen, and pharmaceutical work into production ML models — the kind that respect measurement, uncertainty, and the physical world they came from.

01.My R&D focus

Battery materials

Cell chemistry, electrolyte behaviour, and degradation — building predictive models for capacity, lifetime, and failure modes.

Hydrogen systems

Production, storage, and catalysis data — connecting experimental measurements to models that guide the next experiment.

Drug discovery

Cheminformatics and pharmaceutical R&D — property prediction, screening, and turning messy assay data into usable datasets.

Scientific data engineering

Lab data ingestion, cleaning, and versioning — the invisible pipeline that turns hard-won measurements into a trustworthy dataset.

02.What the science brings

Chemistry taught me habits that most software engineers have to learn the hard way. First-principles thinking — breaking a problem down to what’s actually happening underneath, instead of pattern-matching to the nearest solution.

Respect for measurement. Error, noise, and confidence are first-class citizens in a lab notebook — a number without its uncertainty is just a rumour. I bring that same discipline to model outputs, benchmarks, and metrics.

Data from the real world is expensive. Every point on a plot cost somebody a day in the lab. That perspective changes how I approach dataset design, active learning, and where a model is allowed to fail.

The through-line to the rest of my work is simple: I’m most useful when a problem sits between the bench and the terminal.
