My story

Three disciplines, learned one after the other, that turned out to be one job. I did not plan it that way — each step happened because the previous one ran into something it could not finish on its own.

First

Chemistry

A BS, then an MS, and years at the bench. Every point on a plot cost somebody a day in the lab — and most of those points ended up in a spreadsheet nobody opened again.

Then

AI engineering

Because that data deserved better than a spreadsheet. Classical ML, then deep learning, then RAG and agents — enough to turn a folder of measurements into something that predicts and can be argued with.

Then

Software engineering

Because a model nobody can call is not a product. APIs, Postgres schemas, worker pipelines, infrastructure as code, deployments — the part that turns a notebook into something a team can actually use on a Monday.

Now

All three at once

Deep-tech R&D startups are sitting on years of experimental data and getting almost nothing out of it — because the people who understand the chemistry cannot build the systems, and the people who can build the systems do not understand the chemistry. That gap needs all three, and it is where I work.
